Your Early Childhood Teaching Career, NSW

A planning hub for returning to work and becoming an Early Childhood Teacher (ECT) in NSW.

This hub is built for one profile: an Australian citizen returning to work after a long career break, who holds an overseas Master's degree in a non-teaching subject and has some experience teaching young children overseas.

An overseas Master's is not itself a recognised Australian teaching qualification — but if universities accept it as at least bachelor-equivalent, it unlocks the fast graduate-entry teaching routes that people without a degree cannot take. Citizenship means fee-free TAFE, Commonwealth supported places and HECS-HELP are all available, so the graduate-entry route can cost very little upfront.

Your Recommended Roadmap

Tick steps as you finish them — progress is saved in this browser only.

  1. 1
    This month

    The NSW check is free for volunteers and students on placement; you upgrade to a paid check (about $105, valid 5 years) once you are employed — a volunteer check cannot be used for paid work (who needs a check, apply). Many employers also ask for an approved first aid course that includes asthma and anaphylaxis (e.g. HLTAID012).

  2. 2
    This month

    Ask each provider's admissions team to confirm: (1) your overseas Master's is accepted as at least bachelor-equivalent for entry; (2) the English-language requirement — published graduate diploma entry scores are typically IELTS 6.5–7.0 or equivalent, and some accept prior study completed in English. Verify with each provider. LANTITE (the national literacy and numeracy test) is generally not required for early-childhood-only courses, but is required for combined Early Childhood & Primary courses (WSU example).

  3. 3
    This month

    TAFE NSW / Smart & Skilled, online with local placement — fee-free for eligible NSW residents, which includes Australian citizens.

    Verify this step may be optional: under the National Regulations a person "actively working towards" an approved qualification can count toward the Certificate III requirement, and that may include an ECT degree. If so, you could start the Grad Dip/MTeach and work as an educator without the Cert III. Confirm with ACECQA and prospective employers before skipping it.

  4. 4
    In parallel

    As a citizen you are eligible for a Commonwealth supported place (about $4,700/yr at most providers in 2026) and can defer it with HECS-HELP. The 2026 NSW ECEC Scholarship Program has two rounds; Round 2 is expected to open in late 2026. Published amounts: up to $5,000 (Cert III), $6,000 (Diploma), $35,000 (ECT degree) (scholarship programs). Verify Round 2 dates, and whether a Graduate Diploma or Master of Teaching qualifies for the ECT amount.

  5. 5
    Within ~6 months

    The Cert III placement (~160 hrs) doubles as a working interview; many students are hired casually by their placement centre. Start earning while you study.

  6. 6
    During your degree

    Teaching students on mandatory placements can apply for a means-tested payment of $338.60 per week (2026 rate); it is taxable income. Apply through your university (SCU example).

  7. 7
    1.5–2 years

    Complete the course part-time while working; obtain NESA conditional, then provisional accreditation.

  8. 8

    Then work toward Proficient Teacher accreditation. ECTs are paid under the Educational Services (Teachers) Award or an enterprise agreement; educators under the Children's Services Award, plus the government-funded Worker Retention Payment, now extended to June 2028 (details).

    Verify current hourly rates in the Fair Work Ombudsman pay guides before comparing pathways — third-party sites disagree, and the Children's Services Award was restructured from 1 March 2026.
